How I went from Luanda to running a law firm and a recruitment business

By Ana Vilhete · 26 November 2026

Slowly, and with a lot of help. I was born in Luanda during Angola's civil war, grew up in Lisbon, and came to London at 16 without good English. Twenty years later I run a recruitment business and co-founded a law firm. Nothing about that was quick.

Luanda and Lisbon

My early years were shaped by the war. When I was about four, my family moved to Lisbon. We didn't have much, and I lived without hot water until I was 13. My parents worked hard in cleaning jobs.

London at 16

I came to London in October 2003, and it was my idea. I had to learn English properly, finish my studies and work out how the system worked. About a year after we arrived, my family became homeless for a while and stayed with a friend of my father's until we could afford rent. I mention it because many students I meet are going through something similar and think it rules them out. It doesn't.

Law school, and finding out I'm dyslexic

I studied Law and Criminology with the help of student loans, working alongside my studies. I only found out I was dyslexic at law school. It explained a lot. I completed the Legal Practice Course and qualified as a solicitor in England and Wales.

Into recruitment, then my own business

In 2015 I joined a tech recruitment business as head of legal, working on GDPR, privacy and confidentiality. In 2018 I moved to a new organisation to learn the commercial side and lead people. In 2020, in the middle of the pandemic, I founded Alium Consultancy. We place people in technology, data and healthcare roles, and we care about who gets a fair shot at a shortlist. I completed my Executive MBA at Warwick Business School in 2022.

Back to law

In April 2024 I co-founded Themis Crown Advocates, a London law firm working in immigration, employment, commercial and charity law. Some of those areas are personal to me. I know what it is to arrive in a country and not understand the rules.

What I tell students

If I can do it, anyone can do it.

I don't mean it's easy. I mean that where you start doesn't decide where you finish. Ask for help early, find a mentor, and don't wait until you feel ready. I mentor students at the University of Birmingham and I'm an Entrepreneur in Residence at the University of Portsmouth because I know how much a good mentor can change things.
