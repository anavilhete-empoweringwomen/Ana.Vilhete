Should lawyers rely on AI? How I use it in a law firm and a recruitment business

By Ana Vilhete · 26 November 2026

Short answer: no. I use AI every week, in both of my businesses, but I don't rely on it. It's a tool, not a lifeline. My clients come to me for my knowledge and for a person who understands what they're going through. A piece of software can't give them that.

I said this to Stylist earlier this year, and people have asked me about it since. Here's how I use it.

Where AI earns its place

I run Alium Consultancy, a tech, data and health recruitment business, and I co-founded Themis Crown Advocates, a law firm. Both businesses involve a lot of admin, and that's where AI helps most.

  • My calendar. Two businesses, speaking, mentoring and three children don't fit into a week by accident. Scheduling tools help me keep it all straight.
  • Checking, not deciding. I use it to double-check information and to go back over case studies linked to matters I'm working on. It's a second pair of eyes, and the decision is still mine.
  • Finding people faster. At Alium, technology sorts CVs by work discipline so we can find the right ones quickly. That gives recruiters more time to actually talk to candidates.

Where it doesn't

Legal problems are rarely neat. Someone facing an immigration refusal, an employment dispute or a charity in trouble needs more than a correct answer. They need someone to understand the whole situation, including the parts they find hard to say out loud.

The stakes are too high for me to solely rely on AI.

Getting it wrong can cost someone their job, their right to stay in the country, or their organisation. That's why the final judgement stays with me and my team.

What I'd tell a client

It's fair to ask any lawyer or recruiter how they use AI. Good questions are:

  • Which parts of my matter, or my hiring process, involve AI?
  • Where does my information go, and is it kept confidential?
  • Who checks the output before it reaches me?

I started my recruitment career as head of legal, working on GDPR, privacy and confidentiality. So I take the second question seriously, and you should too.

What I'd tell a founder

Use AI to remove the jobs that drain you, and keep the jobs that make you valuable. For me, that's judgement and relationships. If a tool starts doing those for you, your clients will notice before you do.

Quick answers
Should lawyers rely on AI?

No. AI is useful for admin and for double-checking information, but legal advice depends on knowledge, judgement and empathy. A client's case is too important to hand to a tool.

How does Ana Vilhete use AI?

She uses AI to organise her calendar, double-check information and re-examine case studies linked to matters she is working on. She does not let it replace her own legal judgement.

Does Alium Consultancy use AI in recruitment?

Alium uses technology to sort and find CVs quickly by work discipline, so recruiters spend more time talking to people. Shortlisting and final decisions stay with people.
