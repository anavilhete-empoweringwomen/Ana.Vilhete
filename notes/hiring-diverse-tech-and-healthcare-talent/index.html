How can employers hire more diverse tech and healthcare talent?

By Ana Vilhete · 26 November 2026

Change the shortlist before you change anything else. Most hiring bias doesn't happen at the interview. It happens earlier, when someone decides whose CV is worth reading. If the shortlist looks the same every time, the hires will too.

I've run Alium Consultancy since 2020, recruiting for technology, data and healthcare roles. These are the things that make the biggest difference.

1. Agree the shortlist rule up front

Before the search starts, agree with the hiring manager that the shortlist will include people from different backgrounds. It's much harder to argue for it at the end, when everyone is tired and wants to pick the familiar option.

2. Look past the usual signals

The same few universities, employers and job titles don't predict who will do the job well. Strong candidates often come through routes a keyword search would filter out. Look at what someone has actually done.

3. Write the criteria down, and use them

Decide what good looks like before you meet anyone. Then score every candidate against the same criteria. "Not quite the right fit" often means "not like us". Ask the person saying it to point to the criterion they mean.

4. Be open to international talent

Many UK employers still treat overseas candidates as too complicated. Sometimes there's real work involved, especially with visas, but it's often less than people assume. I'm a solicitor as well as a recruiter, and a good hire can be lost simply because nobody asked the right question early enough.

5. Make the process humane

Tell people what to expect, give feedback, and move quickly. Candidates who have been overlooked before can tell when they're being considered properly.

Diverse teams don't happen by accident. Someone has to decide they matter, before the first CV is read.

Quick answers
How do you build a diverse shortlist?

Agree before you search that the shortlist must include candidates from different backgrounds, look beyond the usual universities and employers, and judge everyone against the same written criteria.
